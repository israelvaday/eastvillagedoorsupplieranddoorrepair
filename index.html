In depth

Questions about nyc door services across NYC

Practical answers from East Village Door Supplier and Door Repair to help you compare door scopes in Manhattan & Brooklyn. Tap a question to expand.

A clear scope identifies the door slab, frame, hardware, weatherstripping, fire rating, and any framing adjustments. Existing damage or structural repairs outside the door scope should be identified before work begins.

Multifamily, commercial, and mixed-use buildings in NYC often require UL-listed fire door assemblies in corridors, stairwells, and certain tenant separations. The written scope should identify the required label, self-closing hardware, and smoke-seal components.

Hardware selection depends on door weight, traffic, security goals, and ADA requirements. Locksets, closers, hinges, panic hardware, and access-control prep should be matched to the door and building use rather than treated as interchangeable.

Many binding, sagging, or latch problems stem from jamb settlement, hinge wear, or strike misalignment — not a failed slab. Structural repair can restore smooth operation and secure closure while preserving original materials when possible.

Accurate on-site measurement accounts for jamb width, header height, floor clearance, swing direction, and existing casing. Pre-war and masonry buildings often need field verification before ordering a slab or custom fabrication.

Settlement, humidity swings, masonry movement, and high-traffic use can affect how doors hang and latch. A responsible scope accounts for the building type — brownstone, loft, multifamily, or storefront — instead of assuming a standard rough opening.

The closeout plan should cover removal of old doors and debris, hardware testing, latch alignment, and a walkthrough against the written scope. Buyer's guide

How to compare door estimates in NYC

A useful door estimate identifies the slab, frame, hardware, code requirements, and exclusions. Use this checklist before approving a scope.

1. Compare the same components. Confirm which door slab, frame, hardware, weatherstripping, and trim are included and which are excluded.

2. Define framing and prep work. Jamb adjustments, header work, threshold replacement, and masonry or drywall repairs should be described instead of grouped into a vague “prep” line.

3. Record door and hardware specs. Note the manufacturer, fire rating, core type, finish, and hardware grade. Product selection affects security, code compliance, and longevity.

4. Clarify code and ADA requirements. Ask whether the quote addresses fire labels, self-closing hardware, clearances, and any DOB or FDNY documentation needed.

5. Plan hardware approval. Confirm who selects locksets, closers, and access-control prep, and when hardware must be locked to protect the schedule.

6. Review access, timing, and cleanup. Occupied buildings need phased scheduling. Confirm debris removal, protection of common areas, and the final walkthrough.

Glossary

Door terms, explained in plain language

These are the terms East Village Door Supplier and Door Repair uses when discussing scope, hardware, and installation expectations.

Door slab — the door panel itself, separate from the frame, hardware, and casing.

Jamb — the vertical frame members the door hangs on; includes the hinge side and strike side.

Rough opening — the framed gap in the wall before the door assembly is installed.

Fire-rated assembly — a UL-listed door, frame, and hardware combination rated for a specific fire duration (e.g. 90 minutes).

Strike plate — the metal plate on the jamb that receives the latch or deadbolt; alignment affects whether the door closes securely.

Door closer — a mechanical device that controls swing speed and ensures self-closing for code compliance.

Weatherstripping — seals around the door perimeter to reduce drafts, noise, and moisture infiltration.

Punch list — the final walkthrough items addressed before cleanup and project closeout.
